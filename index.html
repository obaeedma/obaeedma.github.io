Profile

Analytical by training, curious by nature.

I'm M A Obaeed — a data analyst who leads people, and a leader who decides with data.

I grew up in Narayanganj, where a love of books, debate and science fairs shaped how I think: ask good questions, check the evidence, then explain it so anyone can follow. That instinct led me to Computer Science & Engineering at BRAC University.

Today I work with Python, SQL, Excel and Power BI to clean messy data, find the patterns that matter and present them clearly. Across 25+ academic and independent projects — from air-quality analysis to data-modelled web platforms and dialectal Bangla NLP research — I've learned that good analysis is as much about care and clarity as it is about code.

Beyond the screen, I lead. I've founded and led clubs, led project teams such as ReWater and OfficeKhoj BD, run events, coordinated sponsors and spoken on stage — learning how people, plans and numbers fit together. And whenever I can, I'm out with a camera — I've explored all 64 districts of Bangladesh. Curiosity is the thread that ties it all together.

Undergraduate Thesis · NLP & Machine Learning

Detecting hate speech in dialectal Bangla.

Dialectal Bangla Hate Speech Detection using Natural Language Processing · BIDWESH dataset

Most Bangla NLP work assumes the standard written form. Online comments don't. This research builds and evaluates models on regional dialects, where vocabulary, spelling and class balance all work against you.

Hardware · Robotics · Team build

Robot Car + Automated Crane

Question
Can one compact machine both drive and lift — a mobile robot that carries its own crane and moves real objects at the tap of a screen?
What I did
We engineered it end to end as a team: a wheeled chassis carrying a 3D-printed crane arm with a geared gripper, motors and servos wired for precise movement, and a tablet controller app to command it. Every part — mechanics, electronics and control — was designed, assembled, tested and refined in the lab until the crane could pick up, carry and place objects reliably.

Team hardware build · ESP32

Water & Gas Safety Prototype

Question
Water overflow and gas leaks are silent, everyday dangers. Can a low-cost device sense them early — and act on its own before anyone gets hurt?
What I did
With a three-person team, we built an automatic safety system around an ESP32: a water-level sensor and an MQ-2 gas sensor watch the environment, while a relay, servo and buzzer respond — controlling the pump and sounding the alarm. We wrote and flashed the firmware in the Arduino IDE, then tested and debugged it on the bench until detection-to-response worked consistently.
